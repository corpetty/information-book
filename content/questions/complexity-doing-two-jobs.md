*Open question · part of [[the-finite-reader|The finite reader]]*

<!-- card -->
The sentence "Inflation eased two-tenths of a point last quarter" needs almost no background, and nobody forwards it. An idea that takes real background to grasp can still spread widely, if a short version of it carries enough feeling.

The book first defined an idea's complexity as the number of things a reader must already know for it to land. Then it caught itself using the word for other things: depth, subtlety, emotional pull. The question was whether one word was covering two variables.

The working answer is yes, and the book splits the word in two. One variable belongs to [[transport|transport]]: how much background must survive each retelling. It sets the [[complexity-ceiling|complexity ceiling]] and separates a [[complex-form|complex form]] from a compressed one. The other belongs to [[selection|selection]]: how an idea scores when someone decides whether to pass it on. The book calls that [[handle-ability|handle-ability]]: emotional load, fit with identity, a clear thing to do. An idea has to pass both filters.

The book marks this provisionally resolved. It is fairly confident in the split, less sure that handle-ability is a single variable, and has yet to test cases where both are at work, such as entertainment with rich worldbuilding.
<!-- /card -->

**Status:** provisionally resolved.

> [!note]- The working answer, in the author's words
> Yes — and the resolution is to split the term along the transport/selection seam. *Transport-complexity* is precondition count: how much outer message (decoding key) must survive a hop for the idea to land intact. A property of the idea-in-transit; this is what Chapter 5 is about. *Selection-complexity* is handle-ability: how the idea scores at a gate — emotional load, identity fit, action affordance. A property of the idea-at-a-gate; this is what Chapter 5b is about. The two come apart in the wild — an idea can be low transport-complexity (cheap to carry) and still die if it scores at no gate, or high transport-complexity but spread anyway if its compressed form is high handle-ability. Splitting the term along the transport/selection seam fixed the slide. Chapter 5 uses *complexity* to mean transport-complexity throughout; Chapter 5b uses *handle-ability* for the selection-side axis. Pressure-tests still open: whether the two axes really come apart cleanly in cases where both are doing real work (entertainment with rich worldbuilding; ritual with elaborated symbol systems).
