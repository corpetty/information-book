*Open question · part of [[compression-and-truth|What shortening does to truth]]*

<!-- card -->
"Coffee raises mortality risk." Read as "in this group of people, at this intake, by this much, pending replication", it can be true. Read as "for you, a lot, settled", it is false. Same words.

That example carries a problem the book had to face. Its early chapters describe a machine in which ideas get shorter as they travel, through [[transport|lossy retelling]] and [[selection|filtering]]. Nothing in that machine mentions truth. A fair reader could shrug: communication has always been lossy, and a low-resolution photograph is still a photograph of the right thing. The claim that something has broken needs more than blur.

Where the book lands: truth lives in the fit between the [[three-layer-message|reading instructions]] a claim was made with and the ones its reader actually uses. Shortening drops the instructions and keeps the claim, so the result can stay roughly true, flip, or turn into a team badge. These are the [[truth-compression-regimes|three outcomes]], and filtering picks which one travels.

The book counts this as provisionally settled. Still open: whether the three outcomes are really two plus one, how close counts as roughly true, and how the answer carries over to [[intersubjective-pipeline|truths that exist because people agree on them]].
<!-- /card -->

**Status:** provisionally resolved.

> [!note]- The working answer, in the author's words
> Truth value is not carried by the inner message (the claim) but by the fit between the idea's outer message — its decoding key — and the key the receiver decodes with. Compression strips the key, not the claim, so it can preserve approximate truth (keys close), invert truth (keys opposed), or render truth orthogonal (the claim is read as an identity flag, not decoded as a claim at all). Which regime occurs is decided by selection: the gate picks the compressed variant matching its criteria. Drafted in truth-compression-and-when-each-wins.md.
