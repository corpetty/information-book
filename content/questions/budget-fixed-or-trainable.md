*Open question · part of [[the-finite-reader|The finite reader]]*

<!-- card -->
Someone fluent in a field can take in a new paper in it far faster than a newcomer, who has to build the background first. Both have the same hours in the day.

The book's early chapters treat a person's [[receiver-budget|limited time and attention]] as a hard limit on which ideas can reach them. The question was whether that limit can move. The book's answer is that part of it can: the hours are fixed, and what each hour carries is trainable. New material that hooks onto what you already know is cheap, so structure you build makes later learning cheaper. Shared vocabulary does the same for a whole culture. Tools of thought fit the same pattern: [[notation-as-tool-of-thought|a good notation]] makes some operations easy.

Two things follow. Training is a job institutions do, one half of the [[preservation-vs-training-pair|pair the book calls preservation and training]]. And capacity only gets used if a person [[want-as-prime-mover|wants to use it]], which is why [[medium-shapes-want|a medium that trains a shallow appetite]] does damage.

The book counts this as provisionally settled. It has not said where the trainable ceiling stops. It also points to [[decompression-on-demand|a faithful AI tutor]] as a conditional way to stretch each hour.
<!-- /card -->

**Status:** provisionally resolved.

> [!note]- The working answer, in the author's words
> Trainable. Education, vocabulary, and accumulated engagement expand the budget. Want is the prime mover of the feedback loop. Operationalized in preservation-vs-training.md as the training function — re-installing the decoding key in receivers — which together with preservation forms the institutional pump the integration project needs.
