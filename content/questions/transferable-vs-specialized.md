> **Open question**

Some preconditions are field-general (statistical literacy, critical reading); others field-specific. Field-general ones make natural bridge nodes between specialist communities. Should the book's prescription tilt toward transferable training?

**Status.** provisionally resolved

**Where the book lands.** The dichotomy was wrong. A bridge node between specialist communities is neither a generalist with field-general preconditions nor a pure specialist, but a deep specialist who has paired their depth with metacognitive flexibility — what double-edged-sword-of-expertise calls versatile expertise. Depth is what gets the bridge admitted as a peer on either side; metacognitive flexibility (structural-analogy habits, paradigm suspension, intellectual humility) is what lets the bridge translate across, and is also what disarms the curse-of-expertise failure modes that are the cognitive substrate of polarization-via-distrust. The book's prescription tilts toward cultivating that paired skill inside specialist communities, not toward generalist training. Drafted in bridge-nodes-and-versatile-expertise.md.
