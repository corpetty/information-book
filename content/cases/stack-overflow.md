For years, a programmer stuck on a problem pasted the error into a search engine and landed on Stack Overflow. Answers there were voted on, edited and ranked by people with earned reputations, and you could see who wrote what. That is strong quality control.

It was also paid for by advertising and venture capital from its launch in 2008. When AI models began answering programming questions directly, readers took the instant model answer over the community one. Traffic fell sharply after 2022, and the site had no reserve to ride out the shock.

The book uses the case to show that good curation and visible provenance are not enough. A [[curation-layer|curation layer]] whose income depends on attention can be [[out-competition-of-carriers|out-competed]] by something cheaper, and here the cheaper thing was an AI answer: the [[compressed-form-as-authority|summary replacing its source]]. What was missing is the book's first design rule, [[funding-decoupling|funding from outside the attention market]]. Compare [[common-law-courts|the courts]], which have it.
