In early 2020 the CDC and the WHO told the public not to wear masks. Two things were bundled in that advice: a wish to keep scarce medical masks for hospitals, and an early reading of the evidence that favored droplets over airborne spread. On April 3, 2020 the CDC reversed and recommended cloth coverings, as airborne transmission became clearer.

That is a field updating on evidence. What reached the public was two confident slogans, "masks don't work" and then "masks work." The reasons for the first advice and the provisional state of the evidence had been stripped out along the way, so the change read as experts lying or flip-flopping.

The book works this case in detail as its clearest example of what goes wrong in the [[bridge-zone|bridge zone]], where specialist findings are retold for everyone. The claim survived each retelling as a [[compressed-form|short form]] and the reading instructions did not ([[three-layer-message|the missing key]]). The result is inversion, one of the [[truth-compression-regimes|three truth regimes]]: a true process decoded into a false belief, here about the institutions themselves. Each slogan was also a [[framing-effect|frame]] that set what the next one would be measured against.
