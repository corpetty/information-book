"Oh, you're so Capricorn." The planetary positions behind that sentence are real measurements: accurate, ancient and well documented. The claim that the positions at your birth shape your personality and predict events was never derived from data and never passed a scientific test. It skipped the stages where findings are measured and checked by other experts, and entered public life further along, through horoscope columns and sun-sign small talk.

The book uses astrology as its standard example of [[manufactured-injection|manufactured content]]: material never measured against reality that joins the flow of information partway down. Once in, it looks like everything else. A horoscope and a psychology finding reach you in about the same shape: short, about your life, fitting a story. The filters that decide what gets published and shared, which the book calls [[selection|selection]], check form and appeal. They have no way to ask where a claim came from, and astrology scores well on the [[memetic-fitness|identity and belonging]] they reward.

The book stresses that this is a stable state, two thousand years old. Astrology has only ever lived in the [[bridge-zone|bridge zone]] between experts and the public. Compare [[pseudo-context|pseudo-context]], the opposite case: real information with its use stripped out.
