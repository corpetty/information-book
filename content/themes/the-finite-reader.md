*Theme 5 of 13 · 14 ideas*

The honest part of the machine. Nobody is to blame for the fact that a lifetime is short and fields are vast. Every textbook, sermon, statute and chart library is a trade between saying it all and being usable, and this theme is about that trade.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[info-time-limit|Chapter 3: The Human Time Budget]], [[optionality-vs-access|Chapter 4: Optionality vs. Access]], [[complexity-virality-tradeoff|Chapter 5: The Complexity / Virality Trade-off]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[receiver-budget|Receiver budget]]: Each person has only so many focused hours to learn new things, though what you already know makes every hour go further.
2. [[budget-fixed-or-trainable|Are receiver budgets fixed or trainable?]] *(open question)*: People cannot add hours to the day, but learning makes each hour of attention hold more, so the limit can be raised.
3. [[abyss|The abyss]]: Any rich field holds far more possible paths than anyone could ever explore, and you only see that once you are good at it.
4. [[complex-form|Complex form]]: The full version of an idea keeps what the short version drops, but it only makes sense to someone who already knows a lot.
5. [[compressed-form|Compressed form]]: The short, easy version of an idea spreads fast because it asks almost nothing of the person hearing it.
6. [[matplotlib-vs-seaborn|matplotlib vs. seaborn]] *(example)*: One charting tool lets you do anything but is hard to learn; another is easy because it has already made most choices for you.
7. [[latin-mass-vs-vernacular|Latin Mass vs. vernacular liturgy]] *(example)*: When the Catholic Mass moved from Latin to local languages, far more people could take part, at some cost in doctrinal precision.
8. [[brief-history-of-time|A Brief History of Time]] *(example)*: Stephen Hawking reached millions of readers by cutting nearly every equation from his physics book, trading exactness for reach.
9. [[plain-language-law|Statutes vs. plain-language legal summaries]] *(example)*: A one-line summary of a law is easy to read and right about the usual case, but wrong about edge cases the full text handles.
10. [[complexity-ceiling|Complexity ceiling]]: The more people an idea has to pass through, the simpler it must become to survive the trip.
11. [[handle-ability|Handle-ability]]: Ideas spread when they are easy to grab: they fit what people already think, stir a feeling, and say what to do.
12. [[complexity-doing-two-jobs|Is 'complexity' doing two jobs?]] *(open question)*: An idea can be hard to understand and an idea can be hard to care about, and those are two separate obstacles to spreading.
13. [[transferable-preconditions|Transferable preconditions]]: General skills like reading statistics and asking for evidence help in every subject, but alone they cannot carry expert knowledge between fields.
14. [[bitcoin-internet-money|Bitcoin: 'internet money' vs. full technical description]] *(example)*: Calling Bitcoin "internet money" leaves out nearly everything that matters, yet it is the only description short enough to reach newcomers.
