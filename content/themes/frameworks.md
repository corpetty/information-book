*Theme 3 of 13 · 21 ideas*

The same rulebook idea, moved inside your own head. Languages, notations, metaphors, forms and learned habits all tilt what you find easy to think. A dozen fields discovered this separately and gave it a dozen names. The evidence says the tilt is real and measurable, and that it is a tilt and no cage.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[thinking-inside-frameworks|Thinking Inside Frameworks]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[framework|Framework (a medium for thought)]]: Any structure people build to think with, such as a language, a form, a metric or a model, quietly shapes what they find easy to think.
2. [[framework-pricing|Framework pricing]] *(mechanism)*: A framework makes some thoughts cheap and others expensive, and across a whole population people mostly think the cheap ones.
3. [[cost-asymmetry|Cost asymmetry]]: Inside any tool or system of notation, some things take one step and others take fifty, and people mostly do the one-step things.
4. [[default-setting|Defaults]]: Whatever a system gives you when you make no choice is what most people end up with, so whoever sets the default sets the outcome.
5. [[columbia-powerpoint|The Columbia slide]] *(example)*: Engineers briefed NASA on possible shuttle damage with bullet-point slides, and the layout buried the caveats that mattered most.
6. [[notation-as-tool-of-thought|Notation as a tool of thought]]: The symbols you write a problem in decide which steps are easy, so a better notation puts harder thoughts within reach.
7. [[calculus-notation|Leibniz's dy/dx and the British lag]] *(example)*: British mathematicians stayed loyal to Newton's way of writing calculus, while the rest of Europe used Leibniz's handier symbols and pulled ahead.
8. [[linguistic-relativity|Linguistic relativity]]: The language you speak nudges what you notice and remember, but it does not decide what you are able to think.
9. [[russian-blues|Russian blues]] *(example)*: Russian has separate words for light blue and dark blue, and its speakers tell those shades apart faster, unless a word task interferes.
10. [[piraha-numbers|Pirahã and exact number]] *(example)*: A people whose language has no number words can still match exact quantities they can see, but slip once the task needs memory.
11. [[influence-not-imprisonment|Influence, not imprisonment]]: The tools you think with tilt your thinking in measurable ways, but careful testing shows they do not trap it.
12. [[framing-effect|Framing effect]]: Describe the same facts in two different ways and people will make two different choices.
13. [[asian-disease-problem|The Asian disease problem]] *(example)*: Describe the same outcomes as lives saved or as lives lost, and most people switch which plan they choose.
14. [[conceptual-metaphor|Conceptual metaphor]]: The comparison you use to describe a problem quietly suggests which solutions make sense.
15. [[crime-metaphor|Crime as beast, crime as virus]] *(example)*: Readers told crime was a beast preying on a city favored tougher enforcement than readers told it was a virus, with the same statistics.
16. [[theory-ladenness|Theory-ladenness and paradigms]]: What an expert sees when looking at evidence depends on the theory they already hold.
17. [[einstellung-effect|Einstellung effect]]: Once you know one way to solve a problem, that way keeps pulling your attention back and hides better ones.
18. [[chess-einstellung|Chess experts and the Einstellung effect]] *(example)*: Chess experts who had found a familiar solution said they were hunting for a better one, while their eyes kept returning to the old one.
19. [[law-of-the-instrument|Law of the instrument]]: People reach for the tool they already have, and then see every problem as one that tool can fix.
20. [[inductive-bias|Inductive bias]]: A system that learns from examples has to make assumptions that go beyond those examples, and the assumptions shape everything it concludes.
21. [[prompt-format-sensitivity|Prompt formatting and the 76-point swing]] *(example)*: Changing only spacing, separators or capital letters in the wording given to an AI model moved its test accuracy by as much as 76 points.
