*Theme 10 of 13 · 7 ideas*

What happens to shared understanding as a network gets big: stories stretch to cover everyone and stop meaning one thing, communities drift apart by distrust, and more connection is sometimes worse.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[myths-scale-and-bureaucracy|Myths, Scale, and Bureaucracy]], [[integration-problem|Chapter 9: The Integration Problem]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[myth-dilution|Myth dilution at scale]] *(mechanism)*: The bigger the group a shared story has to hold together, the simpler the story must get, and the more ways people can read it.
2. [[network-segmentation|Network segmentation]]: A huge group can repeat the same slogan while its subgroups each mean something different by it, so it is already several groups underneath.
3. [[echo-chambers-vs-bubbles|Echo chambers vs. bubbles (Nguyen)]]: Never hearing the other side is one thing; being taught to distrust anyone outside your group is another, and small groups drift toward the second.
4. [[polarization-via-distrust|Polarization via distrust]]: Two groups that start slightly apart can drift far apart just by sensibly giving less weight to evidence from people they trust less.
5. [[zollman-effect|The Zollman effect]]: Wiring a group together more tightly can make it less accurate, because everyone hears early, shaky results and settles on an answer too soon.
6. [[inverted-u|Inverted-U relationship]]: For things like group size and how connected people are, the best amount sits in the middle, and both too little and too much hurt.
7. [[integration-vs-fragmentation|Part IV: integration or fragmentation?]] *(open question)*: Retreating into small trusted communities does not fix a noisy information world, so the harder task is getting real knowledge across between groups.
