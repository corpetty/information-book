*Theme 11 of 13 · 9 ideas*

The political economy. A filter that follows rules can be tuned, tuning has an owner, and the owner has a business model. This theme covers how that plays out, and a map of the ways a filter can be turned against its purpose.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[political-economy-of-attention|Chapter 10: Political Economy of Attention]], [[capture-taxonomy|Capture Taxonomy]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[engagement-equilibrium|Engagement-maximization as a captured equilibrium]] *(mechanism)*: A feed that sells your time keeps showing whatever holds you longest, and nobody has to intend any harm for that to happen.
2. [[orwell-huxley-axis|The Orwell/Huxley axis]]: A society can lose the truth by having it banned, or by having it buried under endless entertainment, and the second is harder to fight.
3. [[cost-shifting|Cost-shifting from producers to consumers]]: When publishing became free, the work of checking what is true did not vanish; it moved onto readers, who have no extra time for it.
4. [[creation-cheap-review-expensive|Creation cheap / review expensive]]: Making content has become far quicker than checking it, so the unchecked pile grows faster than anyone can sort it.
5. [[out-competition-of-carriers|Out-competition of institutional carriers in attention markets]]: Careful journalism and scholarship cost money to make while feed content costs platforms nothing, so depth loses the audience without anyone ever attacking it.
6. [[self-vs-external-capture|Self-capture vs. external capture]]: An institution can be bent by outsiders gaming its rules or by its own way of making money, and the second is harder to undo.
7. [[capture-taxonomy|Capture taxonomy (substrate × source × target asymmetry)]]: Anything that filters what people see or learn can be turned against its purpose; the questions are which filter, by whom, and how fixable.
8. [[consumer-key-vs-surface-capture|Consumer-key vs. surface capture]]: Rigging what people are taught does deeper, longer-lasting damage than rigging what they are shown, because you cannot easily unteach a person.
9. [[capture-asymmetry|Capture asymmetry (training vs. preservation)]]: A corrupted library can be rebuilt from other sources, but people trained to read wrongly are slow to retrain, so corrupted teaching is usually worse.
