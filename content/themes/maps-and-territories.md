*Theme 4 of 13 · 17 ideas*

A biased picture misleads whoever reads it. A picture that gets acted on does something stronger: forests are replanted to match the table, prices move to match the formula, people change to match the category. This is where framework bias stops being a quirk of thought and becomes a force in the world, and it ends with what to do about it.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[thinking-inside-frameworks|Thinking Inside Frameworks]], [[intersubjective-truth|Chapter 8b: Intersubjective Truth]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[map-and-territory|The map is not the territory]]: A model or a summary is a simplified picture of something, and trouble starts when people forget what the picture left out.
2. [[legibility|Legibility]]: To manage something large, you first simplify it into what can be written down, and whatever cannot be written down stops counting.
3. [[scientific-forestry|Scientific forestry and the Normalbaum]] *(example)*: German foresters replanted their woods to match a table of timber yields, and after one good harvest the simplified forests began to fail.
4. [[classification-torque|Classification torque]]: When official categories do not fit the people sorted into them, the categories hold and the people are the ones who bend.
5. [[apartheid-classification|Apartheid race classification]] *(example)*: South Africa's government sorted every person into a legal race category that decided where they could live and work, even splitting families.
6. [[dsm-and-rdoc|The DSM and RDoC]] *(example)*: Psychiatry's manual defines illnesses by agreed lists of symptoms, and in 2013 a US research institute said its studies would move away from them.
7. [[framework-feedback|Framework feedback]] *(mechanism)*: When people act on a framework's picture of the world, the world changes, sometimes to match the picture and sometimes to break it.
8. [[goodharts-law|Goodhart's law]]: Once people are rewarded for hitting a number, they find ways to hit it that no longer deliver what the number was meant to track.
9. [[ai-benchmarks|AI benchmarks as milestones]] *(example)*: AI researchers compete on a small set of shared tests, and higher scores on them get read as progress toward general AI.
10. [[performativity|Performativity]]: A model that enough people use to make decisions stops merely describing the world and starts pushing the world toward itself.
11. [[black-scholes|Black-Scholes and the option market]] *(example)*: A formula for pricing options fit the market badly at first, then traders adopted it and prices moved to match, until the 1987 crash.
12. [[gaussian-copula|The Gaussian copula and 2008]] *(example)*: Investment banks shared one formula for agreeing on values and booking profits, so its blind spot about a housing-wide downturn became everyone's blind spot.
13. [[looping-effects|Looping effects]]: People who are put in a category react to being put there, and their reaction changes what the category contains.
14. [[conways-law|Conway's law]]: The things an organization builds end up shaped like the organization's own internal lines of communication.
15. [[path-dependence|Path dependence and lock-in]]: An early choice, even an arbitrary one, can become nearly impossible to change once everyone has built their habits around it.
16. [[qwerty|QWERTY and the disputed lock-in story]] *(example)*: The standard keyboard layout is the best-known example of an early choice that stuck, though whether a better layout really lost is disputed.
17. [[framework-rotation|Framework rotation]]: The way to see how one way of looking is bending your view is to look a second way and compare.
