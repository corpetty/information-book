*Theme 7 of 13 · 6 ideas*

Most of the book treats ideas as reports about a world that does not care what we think. Some truths are different: they exist because enough people hold them. For those, the chain of retellings is wired into the thing itself.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[case-studies-and-three-realities|Chapter 2: Case Studies and Three Realities]], [[intersubjective-truth|Chapter 8b: Intersubjective Truth]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[three-realities|Three realities (Harari)]]: Some things are true whatever anyone thinks, some only inside one mind, and some only because enough people agree, like money or laws.
2. [[truth-generators|Truth generators (reality vs. agreement)]]: Two statements can both be true while one is made true by the world and the other only by people continuing to agree.
3. [[constitutive-transmission|Constitutive transmission]] *(mechanism)*: When something is real only because people agree on it, every retelling adds to or drains the agreement, so spreading the news changes the thing.
4. [[constitutive-regimes|Constitutive regimes (convergent / divergent / hollow)]]: A shared agreement like money or law can hold together, split into rival versions, or survive in words only while people stop acting on it.
5. [[reification|Reification]]: People forget that money, markets and laws are arrangements humans made and could remake, and start treating them like facts of nature.
6. [[intersubjective-pipeline|What does the pipeline do to intersubjective truths?]] *(open question)*: Retelling can garble a fact but never change it, while for things true only by agreement, like money, the retelling helps decide what is true.
