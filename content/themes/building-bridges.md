## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[transferable-vs-specialized|Transferable vs. specialized preconditions]] *(open question)*: Carrying knowledge between fields takes a deep specialist who has learned to step outside their own field's habits, and general thinking skills alone fall short.
2. [[curse-of-expertise|The curse of expertise]]: Knowing one field very deeply can make it harder to see, or take seriously, anything that does not fit that field's way of thinking.
3. [[versatile-expertise|Versatile expertise]]: The people who can carry hard ideas between fields are deep specialists who also know their own way of seeing is only one of several.
4. [[preservation-vs-training-pair|Preservation vs. training (paired institutional functions)]]: Keeping hard knowledge alive takes two jobs at once: storing the full original somewhere, and continually teaching people how to read it.
5. [[institutional-decay-rates|Differential decay rates (preservation vs. training)]]: A neglected library can sit intact for decades, but skilled people must be trained fresh every generation, so teaching fails first.
6. [[curation-layer|Curation layer]]: Let anyone contribute, then use open, layered review to sort the good from the bad, as Wikipedia does.
7. [[funding-decoupling|Funding decoupling from attention markets]]: An institution that does slow, careful work has to be paid in some way that does not depend on winning people's attention.
8. [[survivable-polarization|Survivable polarization (design for partial trust)]]: Build institutions that still work when some people distrust them, by letting doubters check the evidence and reasoning for themselves.
9. [[common-law-courts|Common-law courts as a curation institution]] *(example)*: Common-law courts have kept hard knowledge alive for centuries by training judges for decades, writing everything down, and being paid for by the public.
10. [[stack-overflow|Stack Overflow: curation without funding decoupling]] *(example)*: A programming Q&A site with strong quality control lost its audience when AI began answering the same questions, and it had no funding to fall back on.
11. [[survival-not-victory|Survival, not victory (integration institutions)]]: Careful institutions cannot beat the attention economy, so the goal is to keep doing their work alongside it without being worn down.
12. [[work-of-generations|Work of generations (institutional timescale)]]: Institutions that keep hard knowledge alive take decades or centuries to build, so rebuilding them is a job for generations.
