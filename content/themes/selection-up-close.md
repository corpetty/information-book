The research on virality, read closely: a handful of accounts doing most of the sharing, people spreading what they say they do not want spread, and the stretch of the chain where expert findings get retold for everyone else.

**The same ground, told as a story:** [[bridge-zone-distortion|Chapter 6: Where It All Gets Fucked Up]], [[emotional-memetics|Chapter 7: Emotional Memetics As The Floor]].

## The ideas

Each one is a short page. Walk them in this order, or start with whichever catches your eye.

1. [[paradox-of-virality|Paradox of virality]]: Most people say they do not want angry, alarming posts to go viral, and those posts go viral anyway.
2. [[superspreader-dynamics|Superspreader dynamics]]: A tiny number of very well-connected accounts do most of the spreading, something that was impossible before networks got this big.
3. [[meme-ecosystem-competition|Meme ecosystem competition]]: How far an idea spreads depends on what else is competing for people's attention at the same moment.
4. [[neutral-drift-baseline|Neutral drift baseline (Yule process)]]: Even if nobody preferred any version of an idea, random copying alone would make a few versions hugely popular and leave most obscure.
5. [[replicator-ontology|Replicator ontology]]: Calling ideas "memes" quietly assumes they are separate units copied like genes, and critics argue people rebuild ideas instead of copying them.
6. [[bridge-zone|Bridge zone]]: Most expert knowledge reaches the public through journalists, popularizers, influencers and chatbots, and that handoff is where it gets reshaped the most.
7. [[covid-mask-guidance|COVID mask guidance: a reversal read as a lie]] *(example)*: Health agencies changed their mask advice as evidence came in, but the public heard two flat slogans and concluded the experts had lied.
8. [[nutrition-reversals|Nutrition science: forty years of monocausal reversals]] *(example)*: For forty years the public has been handed one confident dietary villain after another, while the science kept saying it was complicated.
9. [[monetary-policy-press|Monetary policy in the financial press]] *(example)*: Coverage of central banking boils a technical subject down to 'the Fed is printing money', and some of the outlets doing it have a stake in the answer.
10. [[pseudo-context|Pseudo-context]]: Trivia, news quizzes and factoid videos give true facts a pretend use after the real use has been stripped away.
