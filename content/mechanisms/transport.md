*Mechanism · part of [[how-ideas-travel|How ideas travel]]*

<!-- card -->
Ten kids sit in a circle and whisper a sentence from ear to ear. What comes out at the end barely resembles what went in. The [[telephone-game|telephone game]] is the simplest picture of what the book calls **transport**: the work of re-encoding an idea for its next audience. A finding becomes a news story, then a headline, then a meme, and every step loses something.

The bare claim is small and usually survives. What falls away is the instructions for reading it: the method, the caveats, the conditions under which it holds. Twenty years of research arrives as [[scientists-say-x|"scientists say X"]], and readers decode it with whatever assumptions they already carry. Losses compound, so the bigger the network an idea must cross, the lower the [[complexity-ceiling|ceiling on how much it can carry]].

The book treats transport as a cost, like friction. It scrambles every idea the same way whatever the idea says, so nobody can aim it. That is the contrast with [[selection|selection]], which decides which ideas travel at all and can be aimed. Transport cannot be repaired away either. The book says lossiness is what abstraction is, so the [[compressed-form|compressed form]] is the price of reach.
