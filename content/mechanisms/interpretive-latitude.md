*Mechanism · part of [[compression-and-truth|What shortening does to truth]]*

<!-- card -->
"Follow the science." "Freedom." "The people." Almost everyone will nod at these, and each person nodding may mean something different. Compare "the Earth goes around the Sun", which can be read a few ways but not many.

The book's account: a claim travels with unstated [[three-layer-message|instructions for how to read it]], and [[transport|retelling]] strips those first. That leaves the reader with no instructions at all, which is different from handing them one particular wrong set. A bare claim supports a whole range of readings, and the more that was stripped, the wider the range. The book calls that range **interpretive latitude**. Shortening a claim therefore does two things. It shifts the typical reading (the [[truth-compression-regimes|three outcomes for truth]]), and it widens the spread of readings across a crowd.

The spread is what matters at scale. A story simple enough for a whole society to hold is one its members can read a dozen incompatible ways, the loop the book calls [[myth-dilution|myth dilution]]. Readings pool by community, and the result is [[network-segmentation|a network split into camps]] that repeat the same words. The book's proposed repair is institutions that [[preservation-vs-training-pair|keep the full version and train people to read it]].
