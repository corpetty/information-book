*Mechanism · part of [[who-owns-the-gates|Who owns the filters]]*

<!-- card -->
You open your phone to check one thing and surface an hour later from a stream of outrage clips you never went looking for. Nobody hid the calm, useful version of the day's news. It lost, at every turn, to whatever kept you scrolling.

The book calls this **engagement maximization as a captured equilibrium**. Engagement counts time spent inside the app: clicks, dwell, return visits. That time is what gets sold to advertisers. Platforms present the number as a measure of what people want, and it is [[goodharts-law|Goodhart's law]] in action: once the number became the target, the [[algorithm-as-selection-engine|ranking algorithm]] tuned to it drifted toward threat, identity and outrage, because those hold attention. Researchers call the resulting gap the [[paradox-of-virality|paradox of virality]]: most people say they would prefer accurate, nuanced content to spread, and the other kind spreads anyway.

No outsider attacked the platform to produce this. Its own business model does the tuning, which the book calls [[self-vs-external-capture|self-capture]]. That is why it is so stable: there is no villain to defeat, only an arrangement to dismantle. One result is that platforms [[out-competition-of-carriers|out-compete the institutions]] that produce depth.
