*Concept · part of [[how-ideas-travel|How ideas travel]]*

<!-- card -->
When NASA sent a golden record into deep space on the Voyager probes, nobody could assume anything about who might find it. So the cover is etched with instructions for building a player. The record had to carry its own manual ([[voyager-golden-record]]).

Douglas Hofstadter's model, from *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, says every message has three layers. The frame says "this is a message, decode me." The outer message is the key: what you need to know already in order to decode it. The inner message is the content. Almost no message carries its own outer layer the way Voyager did. A physics paper assumes you can read physics. A meme assumes you share the reference.

The book uses the model to say what retelling loses. The content is small and survives. The key falls away, so a long research program arrives as [[scientists-say-x|"scientists say X"]], and the reader decodes it with whatever key they happen to hold ([[transport]]). A true claim read with the wrong key produces a false belief ([[truth-compression-regimes|the three truth regimes]]). How large and rare an idea's key is sets how far it can travel ([[complexity-ceiling]]). A [[theory-ladenness|scientific paradigm]] is a shared key of this kind.
