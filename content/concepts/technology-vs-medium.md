*Concept · part of [[the-medium|The medium sets the rules]]*

<!-- card -->
The same phone can show you a feed of short posts ranked by reactions, a two-hour podcast, or a scientific paper. The hardware does not change. What changes is everything around it: the length limits, the ranking rules, what gets rewarded, what people have come to expect. Neil Postman, writing about television in 1985, drew this line. A technology is the machine. A medium is the social and intellectual environment that grows up around how the machine is used.

When the book says "medium" it always means the second. A medium, in its sense, is the set of rules that decide [[option-space|what forms an idea can take]], [[selection|what gets passed on]] and, over time, [[medium-shapes-want|what audiences learn to want]]. That makes a medium one kind of [[framework|framework]]: a human-made structure that people think inside.

The distinction guards against blaming hardware for a pattern of use. One technology can host very different media, so the rules were somebody's decision and could have been made differently. They rarely look that way from inside. A character limit or a [[default-setting|default]] reads as the shape of the world. An [[algorithm-as-selection-engine|engagement-ranked feed]] is only one of the media a phone can host.
