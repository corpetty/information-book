*Concept · part of [[frameworks|Thinking inside frameworks]]*

<!-- card -->
A form arrives with one box already ticked. Most people leave it ticked. The person who designed the form has decided the result for the majority without forbidding anything.

A **default** is the option a [[framework]] supplies when you do not choose: the pre-ticked box, the standard chart, the reference point a question is asked from, the template an AI model is prompted with. In the best-known experiment the same outcomes were described once as lives saved and once as lives lost, and most people's choice flipped with the description ([[asian-disease-problem]]). Nothing about the facts had changed. The starting point had, which is the [[framing-effect|framing effect]].

The framework strand of the book fits in one line: the framework sets the default, and defaults drive outcomes. A default is the cheapest item on the [[framework-pricing|price list]], because taking it costs no decision at all. It is also the quietest place to steer people, which is why it matters for [[deployment-capture|how AI systems are configured]] and for any [[manipulation-surface|surface a manipulator can tune]].
