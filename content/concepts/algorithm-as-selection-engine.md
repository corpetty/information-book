*Concept · part of [[the-medium|The medium sets the rules]]*

<!-- card -->
A platform defends its feed by saying it shows people what they want to see. The book reads that defense as more revealing than intended. It concedes that the feed ranks, that the ranking has criteria, and that the criteria could have been chosen differently.

The book's name for this is the **algorithm as selection engine**. A recommendation algorithm is no neutral pipe. It is a [[selection|gate]] with adjustable weights, re-tuned continuously by its owner. The book cites an audit of the Twitter/X algorithm which found it amplified political content people said they did not want. That gap is the [[paradox-of-virality|paradox of virality]], which the book takes as evidence of whose gate is choosing.

"Who tunes the gates" becomes a literal question, which is why the book treats [[engagement-equilibrium|engagement-tuned ranking]] as a matter of political economy. And the algorithm is the fast, live end of a continuum whose slow end is the medium's fixed format, the [[option-space|menu of forms an idea can take]]. Both belong to the [[manipulation-surface|manipulation surface]]. The book applies the same lens to language models, whose owners [[llm-design-moment-collapse|hold the gate, the menu and the content at once]].
