*Concept · part of [[made-by-agreement|Truths made by agreement]]*

<!-- card -->
In 1971 a presidential signature ended the dollar's convertibility into gold. For an instant everyone could see that money is a decision. Then the moment passed, and money went back to reading like physics.

**Reification** is the old name the book uses for that long in-between state: treating a made thing as a found thing. "Money just is valuable." "The market decided." "The law is the law." Each sentence files a [[truth-generators|truth made by agreement]] under truths made by reality. It is a close sibling of mistaking [[map-and-territory|the map for the territory]].

The book argues the filing error is far from innocent. An agreement that looks like a law of nature has a maker nobody audits, and that is the cover anyone who has captured the agreement-making wants. It works best on an agreement that has [[constitutive-regimes|gone hollow]], which still looks like a working one.

The book pairs it with the opposite slip, the "orthogonal" case among its [[truth-compression-regimes|truth regimes]], where a factual claim is read as a team badge. That slip makes an answerable question unanswerable. Reification makes a renegotiable arrangement look fixed. So saying out loud "this is a decision" counts, in the book, as a political act.
