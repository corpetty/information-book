*Concept · part of [[made-by-agreement|Truths made by agreement]]*

<!-- card -->
A dog, a toothache and a twenty-dollar bill are all real, in three different ways. The dog is there whether or not anyone believes in it. The toothache exists in one mind only. The twenty buys groceries because enough people agree that it does.

Yuval Noah Harari, in *Nexus*, names these **three realities**: objective, subjective and intersubjective. Currencies, nations, laws, brands and religions belong to the third. They have real consequences (you can be arrested for breaking a law), and those consequences exist because a network of people agrees they do.

The book borrows the split because its account of how ideas travel treats each kind differently. A claim about the objective world can be garbled on its way to an audience while [[truth-generators|the fact itself stays put]], which is what a correction appeals to in a case like [[arsenic-life|arsenic-life]]. Subjective states mostly do not travel at all. Shared agreements are the odd case: [[constitutive-transmission|passing them along helps make them real]]. For those, the danger is someone steering how the agreement gets made, often behind [[reification|the belief that it is a fact of nature]]. The book sorts the outcomes into [[constitutive-regimes|three states an agreement can be in]].
