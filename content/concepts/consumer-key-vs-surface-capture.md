Suppose a newspaper's front page is rigged. Swap the editor and tomorrow's page is different. Now suppose a school curriculum is rigged for twenty years. Swapping the curriculum fixes nothing for the people already taught: they read every new fact through what they learned.

The book's capture taxonomy turns this into a rule. Capture means tuning a filter against what it was meant to serve. Some filters only shape the surface a person meets: what a feed ranks, how an AI tool is configured, what an archive holds. Others install the equipment people (or AI models) read with: schooling, an AI's [[corpus-capture|training data]], the [[objective-capture|goal it was trained toward]]. The book calls the second kind consumer-key capture and argues it is harder to recover from. The damage sits in the reader, and recovery takes at least a generation.

The rule generalizes an earlier claim, the [[capture-asymmetry|asymmetry between captured training and captured archives]]. It also sets a priority for [[llm-as-capability-extender|institutions that want trustworthy AI]]: defend training data and objectives first, and accept weaker defenses on [[deployment-capture|configuration]], which can be re-tuned cheaply. The book notes that the commercial landscape does the reverse.
