*Concept · part of [[building-bridges|Building bridges that last]]*

<!-- card -->
Anyone can edit Wikipedia. What keeps it usable is everything behind the edit button: layers of review, editors with track records, a public history of every change. Stack Overflow's reputation scores and the preprint-then-peer-review route in science follow the same pattern.

The book calls this a **curation layer**, a term from the author's earlier essay "The Democratization Paradox". It keeps the gatekeeping job (separating signal from noise) and drops the entry barrier (who is allowed to take part). The need for it comes from a lopsided cost: [[creation-cheap-review-expensive|making content is cheap and checking it is expensive]].

In the book's prescription the curation layer is the institutional home for [[versatile-expertise|versatile experts]]. Without them it is bureaucracy without judgment. It is also only a form: the [[preservation-vs-training-pair|preserving and training]] still has to happen inside it. Its weak point is trust. Once a community distrusts the institution, [[polarization-via-distrust|its calls are discounted like any outsider's]]. So the book says it must be built for [[survivable-polarization|partial trust]], open to audit by people who start from different assumptions (a kind of [[framework-rotation|framework rotation]] at institutional scale), and [[funding-decoupling|funded from outside the attention market]].
