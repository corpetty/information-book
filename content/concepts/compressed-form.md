"Bitcoin is internet money." To follow that sentence you need to know what money is and what the internet is, and nothing more. The book calls a sentence like this the **compressed form** of an idea: the version that needs almost no background to land. "Climate change is real" and "inflation is bad" are others. [[bitcoin-internet-money|The Bitcoin pair]] is the book's standard example.

The short version costs almost nothing from a person's [[receiver-budget|limited time and attention]]. It plugs into what they already think, leaves a feeling, and can be passed on without being understood. That makes it the version that travels, and the only one with a chance of reaching someone who has not done the homework. The price is loss. What usually falls away is the [[three-layer-message|instructions for reading the claim]]: its conditions, caveats and scope.

The book does not cast the short version as the villain, and often it stays [[truth-compression-regimes|roughly true]]. But with only a feeling or two to grab hold of, it is [[manipulation-surface|easier to weaponize]] than the [[complex-form|full version]] it came from, and across a large audience it can be [[interpretive-latitude|read many different ways]].
