A propaganda team studies what a platform's [[algorithm-as-selection-engine|feed algorithm]] rewards, then crafts posts built to win there. That is one way a platform gets bent away from informing people. The other needs no outsider: the platform sells attention to advertisers, so its feed already rewards whatever holds attention.

The book calls the first external capture and the second self-capture. Capture, in its usage, means tuning a filter against the thing it was supposed to serve. External capture is an arms race, which O'Connor and Weatherall describe as lopsided: every barrier becomes the attackers' next target. Still, there is someone to expose, regulate or fight.

Self-capture has no such opponent. The captured state is the institution running normally, as in the [[engagement-equilibrium|engagement equilibrium]]. The book argues this makes it more stable: you can fight a captor, you cannot fight an equilibrium. It has to be displaced, or the money flow holding it in place has to change.

In practice the two combine. Self-capture lays down a slope that outsiders ride, and the [[capture-taxonomy|capture taxonomy]] treats that composite as the common case. [[llm-design-moment-collapse|AI models]] repeat the pattern with more levers. The book admits its stability argument may prove too much.
