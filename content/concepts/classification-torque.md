<!-- card -->
Two twins are born in South Africa under apartheid. Officials file one as "coloured" and the other as "African." From then on the law treats them as different kinds of person, with different places to live and work ([[apartheid-classification]]).

Geoffrey Bowker and Susan Leigh Star call the force at work here **torque**: the twisting that happens when a life and a classification system pull in different directions and the system has the power. In their words, lives are "twisted, even torn" in the attempt to force one into the other. They also point out that classification systems behave like infrastructure. Nobody notices them while they work, and charged agendas often arrive presented as purely technical.

Torque is what [[legibility]] costs the people being made legible. It is a [[framework]] meeting something it has no box for, and winning. Categories applied to people have a further twist, because people respond to being sorted ([[looping-effects|looping effects]]). The book's worry about [[reification|treating made things as found things]] applies in full: a category that looks like nature is one nobody thinks to audit. The countermeasure is to question the categories as hard as the conclusions ([[framework-rotation]]).
