For centuries, astronomers who believed the planets moved in perfect circles around the Earth kept finding that the planets did not quite cooperate. Each time, they added another small circle on top of the old ones. The system never failed on its own terms, because there was always one more circle to add.

N. R. Hanson called this the **theory-ladenness** of observation: what a scientist sees depends on the concepts brought to the looking. Thomas Kuhn widened it to whole communities. A paradigm tells a field which puzzles are worth solving and sets awkward results aside until they pile up into a crisis. A related point, from Duhem and Quine, is that evidence never tests one idea alone, so the framework decides which part gets blamed when a prediction fails.

In the book's vocabulary a paradigm is a specialist community's shared reading instructions, the key its members decode every result with ([[three-layer-message|the three layers of a message]]). That is why findings cross between fields so badly ([[transferable-preconditions]]), and why deep training can blind as well as equip ([[curse-of-expertise|the curse of expertise]], [[einstellung-effect|the Einstellung effect]]). It is a [[framework]] at the scale of a profession.
