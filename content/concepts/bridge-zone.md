Eggs are killing you. Eggs are fine. It's sugar. It's seed oils. The whiplash comes less from nutrition science, whose own posture was closer to "it's complicated," than from the handoff between the scientists and you.

The book calls that handoff the **bridge zone**: the stretch between deep specialists and the general public, staffed by journalists, popularizers, influencers, teachers working outside their specialty, and now AI summarizers. Almost nobody reads the epidemiology paper. They read someone's summary, so this is where most people's picture of expert knowledge gets made.

The telephone game garbles a sentence by accident. Here the reshaping is deliberate. Someone is paid to make the finding land, and landing means winning clicks and approval, the contest the book calls [[selection|selection]]. The hedged result becomes [[scientists-say-x|"scientists say X"]]. A [[framing-effect|choice of frame]] or a [[conceptual-metaphor|vivid metaphor]] does its work on a general audience here. The book holds that most of these people do their jobs well, and the jobs often reward something other than accuracy. They answer to audiences and employers far more than to the specialists they translate.

[[power-posing|Power posing]] and [[pseudo-context|pseudo-context]] are typical failures. The book's proposed repair is [[versatile-expertise|specialists trained to translate]].
