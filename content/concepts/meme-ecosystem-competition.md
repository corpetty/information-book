<!-- card -->
Open a phone feed and every post in it is competing with every other post for the same few seconds of your attention. A joke that would travel far on a slow day can sink without trace when something louder is going around.

That is **meme ecosystem competition**. Ideas do not spread in isolation. How fast one catches on, and how fast it dies, depends on what else is circulating at the time. One paper the book draws on, which models memes the way epidemiologists model infections, names this as the thing its model leaves out: the infection and decay rates would really be functions of the other memes already present.

So [[memetic-fitness|fitness]] is relative to the current crowd. An idea's [[handle-ability|handle-ability]], how easily people can grab it and use it, is also a comparison: easy next to what. Because every person has a [[receiver-budget|limited budget of attention]], [[selection|selection]] is always a contest among whatever showed up today.

The book treats this as a refinement of its account. Ideas still compete inside rules that somebody set, such as a feed's [[algorithm-as-selection-engine|ranking algorithm]], and who sets those rules is the question the book cares about.
