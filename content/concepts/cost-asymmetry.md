*Concept · part of [[frameworks|Thinking inside frameworks]]*

<!-- card -->
In one popular charting library, a scatter plot with a trend line is a single line of code. A chart the library's authors did not anticipate can take an afternoon. Nothing stops you making the unusual chart. You will just make fewer of them, and so will everyone else who uses that library.

**Cost asymmetry** is the plain fact that a [[framework]] makes some operations cheap and others laborious. Leibniz's way of writing calculus carries a key rule in the symbols themselves, so the rule is nearly free to use, and Newton's dots do not ([[calculus-notation]]). A slide format makes a ranked list of fragments cheap and a sustained argument costly ([[columbia-powerpoint]]).

It is the working part of [[framework-pricing|framework pricing]], and it is the same trade the book finds in every designed artifact: what is easy to reach was paid for with what is hard to reach ([[matplotlib-vs-seaborn]]). Every reader has a [[receiver-budget|limited budget of attention]], so cheap wins unless someone has a reason to pay.
