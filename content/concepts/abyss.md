*Concept · part of [[the-finite-reader|The finite reader]]*

<!-- card -->
A beginner cook learns thirty recipes and feels that cooking is a finite skill: a few hundred dishes and you are done. A chef sees an unbounded space of techniques, ingredients and cuisines, where every answer opens ten new questions. The beginner thinks the room has walls.

The book calls that space **the abyss**: everything a complex field could contain, when that is more than any person or machine could ever search. A plotting library, however huge, is finite and can be mastered. Everything you could express by turning data into a picture has no top, and nobody commands it. You have to be competent to see the abyss at all.

Experts cope by compressing. Their patterns and instincts are a lossy map of ground too large to walk, which ties the abyss to the book's [[compressed-form|compressed forms]] and to [[receiver-budget|everyone's limited attention]]. It is the [[map-and-territory|map and territory]] idea at full strength: in such a field a map is all anyone has. Depth that forgets this hardens into the [[curse-of-expertise|curse of expertise]]. Depth that remembers it becomes [[versatile-expertise|the humble, bridging kind]]. The book's worry about [[ai-as-new-node|AI systems]] is that they hold the map with no sense of its edge, which lets a fluent summary pass as the answer.
