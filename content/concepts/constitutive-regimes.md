<!-- card -->
In the first weeks of a hyperinflation, everyone still calls the banknotes money. They also spend their wages within hours of being paid, because nobody wants to hold the notes. The word survives while the behavior that made it true has gone.

The book calls this hollow constitution, one of three states that a [[truth-generators|truth made by agreement]] can be in. In convergent constitution, people's readings land close enough together to add up to one agreement: most money and most law, most of the time. In divergent constitution, camps affirm the same words as different agreements. A constitutional crisis is the type case, with both sides waving the same document. That is [[network-segmentation|a network splitting into segments]], happening to the agreement itself.

These are siblings of the book's [[truth-compression-regimes|three outcomes for factual claims]]. A fact can be gotten wrong. An agreement has no outside fact to be wrong about, so it fails by splitting or by emptying out. The hollow state is the one [[reification|reification]] suits best, since an agreement that has stopped working still looks like a working one. The book flags the three-part scheme as provisional: hollow may turn out to be where divergence ends up.
