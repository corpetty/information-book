*Concept · part of [[how-people-believe|How people actually believe things]]*

<!-- card -->
In a finding Hugo Mercier cites, supporters of Donald Trump were shown corrections to false claims. They accepted the corrections. Their support did not move. If the false beliefs had been holding up the support, removing them should have shaken it.

Mercier's reading, in *Not Born Yesterday*, reverses the usual story, in which content shapes belief and belief drives behavior. He argues that behavior is mostly settled upstream, by group identity, status and material interest, and that people then shop for content that justifies what they were going to do anyway. The belief is the alibi for a choice that came first.

Mercier calls this the **justification market**. The book treats it as a deeper challenge to its own picture, in which [[selection|selection]] installs beliefs, and thinks he is partly right. Its answer is that both arrows run. Belief leads where behavior depends on information, as in public health. Justification leads where behavior depends on identity. Such beliefs are often [[reflective-vs-intuitive-belief|reflective]]. For repair, supply is only half the job: even a [[versatile-expertise|bridging expert]] meets an audience whose demand was shaped by [[medium-shapes-want|years of media habit]]. Whether this is a separate mechanism from the [[engagement-equilibrium|engagement equilibrium]], the book leaves open.
