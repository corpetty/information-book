*Concept · part of [[how-ideas-travel|How ideas travel]]*

<!-- card -->
Picture a single rumor. It gets retold in a thousand slightly different ways. Only the versions short enough to fit in a text message get sent. Of those, people forward the juiciest. The middle step is easy to miss, and the book calls its result the **option space**: the menu of forms an idea can take at a given stage.

Three things happen at every stage. [[transport|Retelling]] scatters an idea into variants. The medium bounds which variants can be expressed. [[selection|Selection]] picks from what is left. Selection only ranks; it never adds to the menu. A 280-character limit does not slow the long, qualified argument down. It takes it off the menu.

That is why the book calls the medium frozen selection: a choice made once that stops looking like a choice. A ranking algorithm can be watched and argued with. A format looks like the shape of the world. So the menu is the most powerful and least visible [[manipulation-surface|place to manipulate]], because an idea the format cannot hold never needs suppressing. The same holds for any [[framework|framework people think inside]]: it supplies [[default-setting|defaults]] and makes [[cost-asymmetry|some moves cheap and others laborious]] before any choosing starts.
