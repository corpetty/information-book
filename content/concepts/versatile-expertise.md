*Concept · part of [[building-bridges|Building bridges that last]]*

<!-- card -->
A chess master looks at a business rivalry and sees positional play. A physicist looks at a market and sees a phase transition. Neither is a generalist. Each is a deep specialist who has trained a second habit: looking for the same structure in an unfamiliar field, and remembering that the home field's way of seeing is one option among several.

The book calls this pairing **versatile expertise**, and argues it is what a working bridge between two specialist communities is made of. Depth gets you taken seriously as a peer on either side. Flexibility lets you translate. General skills like statistical literacy (the book's first guess, [[transferable-preconditions|transferable preconditions]]) fall short on their own, because an outsider cannot follow the specialists' arguments and gets discounted at the door.

Depth without the flexibility is the [[curse-of-expertise|curse of expertise]]. Depth with it is [[framework-rotation|framework rotation]] practiced by one person: holding more than one way of seeing and knowing which one you are using. The same flexibility is what lets a bridge survive [[polarization-via-distrust|being distrusted by both sides]]. The book admits such people are slow and costly to produce, and that they need an institution, a [[curation-layer|curation layer]], to work inside.
