A tax form has boxes. Whatever about your year does not fit a box does not exist, as far as the tax office is concerned. A slide deck has bullets. A census has a short list of categories. A spreadsheet has a grid.

Each of these is a **framework**: a structure somebody built that other people then do their thinking inside. Languages, notations, metaphors, classifications, models, metrics, tools and org charts all count. The book treats a framework as a [[technology-vs-medium|medium]] for thought. A platform's rules decide which posts can win before anyone posts. A framework decides which thoughts come easily before anyone thinks. Both are choices made once, in advance, that stop looking like choices (the [[option-space|option space]] drawn ahead of time).

A framework works through [[framework-pricing|prices]], through [[default-setting|defaults]], and through what it makes [[legibility|visible]]. The evidence says the result is [[influence-not-imprisonment|a lean, with no lock on the door]]. It becomes serious when people act on it and [[framework-feedback|the world is rebuilt to match]].
