*Concept · part of [[the-finite-reader|The finite reader]]*

<!-- card -->
Picture someone who is good with statistics, reads carefully, and always asks for the evidence. Hand them a particle-physics paper. Their skills are real, and they still cannot follow the argument. They read each sentence one at a time, hold nothing as a larger unit, and run out of working memory before it resolves.

Those general skills are what the book calls **transferable preconditions**: background that helps with many subjects at once. The book's first guess was that people trained in them could act as bridges, picking up any specialist's work and translating it onward. The guess failed on two counts. General skills do not supply the expert's trained way of seeing a field. And the receiving community [[polarization-via-distrust|discounts an outsider]], however careful.

So [[transferable-vs-specialized|the book revised its answer]]. General skills matter as the paired skill, not as the bridge itself. The people who can move hard ideas between fields are deep specialists who have added mental flexibility on top, which the book calls [[versatile-expertise|versatile expertise]]. Depth without that flexibility is [[curse-of-expertise|the curse of expertise]]. The book still counts teaching these skills widely as a necessary part of [[preservation-vs-training-pair|training readers]].
