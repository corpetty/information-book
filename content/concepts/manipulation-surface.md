*Concept · part of [[the-medium|The medium sets the rules]]*

<!-- card -->
A slogan is easier to weaponize than a careful argument. The argument has too many handles to push in one direction. The slogan has one or two emotional charges, and an attacker needs only the right one. That is the book's first account of the **manipulation surface**, whatever an attacker can exploit: the bigger the network, the lower the [[complexity-ceiling|ceiling on what ideas can carry]], and the closer everything sits to raw feeling.

The second account is the one the book argues for, and it is worse. The [[selection|gates that decide what spreads]] run on criteria, and criteria can be set. Nobody has to craft a perfect viral post. Tune what the gate rewards, and every receiver downstream does the rest. A [[algorithm-as-selection-engine|recommendation algorithm]] has adjustable weights its owner can retune. The book defines manipulation as tuning a gate's criteria against truth, upstream of any particular lie.

The deepest version leaves no fingerprints. Shape the medium itself, its [[option-space|menu of allowed forms]] and its [[default-setting|defaults]], and the unwanted idea is never demoted or deleted. It is just hard to say. Later chapters sort these into kinds of [[capture-taxonomy|capture]] and separate outside attackers from [[self-vs-external-capture|a platform's own business model]].
