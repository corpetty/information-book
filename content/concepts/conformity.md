<!-- card -->
Everyone at the table agrees the new manager is hopeless. You arrived with no opinion. By dessert you have one, and nobody gave you any evidence. What moved you was the agreement itself.

**Conformity** is adopting a belief because your peers hold it. In the book it is one of the social criteria by which ideas get passed on or dropped, the sorting it calls [[selection|selection]]. It differs from the content-side features of [[memetic-fitness|memetic fitness]], such as emotional punch or a clear call to action. Conformity ignores what the claim says and weighs who already agrees.

Cailin O'Connor and James Owen Weatherall's network models, in *The Misinformation Age*, show what this does at scale. Conformity pressure can lock a community onto a belief, true or false, and seal it off from evidence that would correct it. Together with [[credibility-weighting|trusting people like us more]], it helps explain how [[false-consensus-rational-agents|careful reasoners can agree on something false]].

The book records a caution. Hugo Mercier reads Asch's classic conformity experiments as showing more resistance to the group than their reputation suggests, part of his case for [[open-vigilance|open vigilance]]. The book treats that as one reading among several.
