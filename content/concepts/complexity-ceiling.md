*Concept · part of [[the-finite-reader|The finite reader]]*

<!-- card -->
Ten kids sit in a circle and pass a sentence from one to the next. What comes out the far end barely resembles what went in. The [[telephone-game|telephone game]] is the book's picture of what happens to any idea that has to cross a large group of people.

Each retelling is done by someone with their own limited time, background and audience, so each one has some chance of dropping or bending part of the idea. The book's illustration: if every retelling keeps the idea intact 90% of the time, ten retellings leave about a one-in-three chance it arrives clean, and a hundred leave almost none. Small losses compound. So the bigger the network an idea must cross, the less it can carry. The book calls that limit the **complexity ceiling**. It is why a twenty-year research program shows up online as [[scientists-say-x|"scientists say X"]].

This is the [[transport|retelling-loss]] half of the book's story. It covers ideas that get passed on, while [[selection|a second mechanism]] decides which ones get passed on at all. Institutions that carry the [[complex-form|full version]] through time can stretch the limit. A low ceiling also leaves a crowd's ideas [[manipulation-surface|easier to weaponize]].
