*How Ideas Get Compressed, Filtered, and Twisted on the Way to You*

A working draft of a book about why complex ideas have such a hard time traveling through modern media, and what can be done about it.

## The problem the book takes on

Think about the last time a real scientific finding reached you. It almost certainly didn't arrive as the study itself. It arrived as a headline, a thirty-second video, a chart someone screenshotted, or a thing a friend repeated at dinner. By the time it reached you it had passed through many hands, and every hand changed it a little.

That re-telling has always happened. What let it stay roughly honest, for most of history, was that moving an idea to a mass audience was slow and expensive: it had to survive peer review, then a journalist, then popularization, then conversation, and each slow step doubled as a quality check. Digital media made moving an idea almost free, which didn't remove the distortion; it removed the *brake*. What now decides which ideas travel is a set of gates tuned to reward emotional punch and group-identity signals over accuracy. The result is an environment where most of what reaches us is oversimplified to the point of being wrong, engineered to provoke a reaction, or manufactured to *look* like a finding without being one. This isn't a story about villains. It's a story about a pipeline whose slow, quality-checking steps were out-competed, without anyone doing the work to replace what they quietly did.

## The shape of the argument

An idea moves from reality out to a general audience through a chain of stages, each a further simplification of the last:

> **what's actually out there → a measurement of it → an expert finding → a news story → a meme**

Two different things happen to it at *every* step, and most explanations of our information mess notice only one. The first is **transport**: the idea gets repackaged for the next stage, and every repackaging drops something, usually not the headline claim (that survives) but the *instructions for how to read it*: the method, the "only under these conditions," the "we're not sure yet." The claim arrives; the manual doesn't. The second is **selection**: at each step a gate decides which ideas move at all, on criteria (publishable, newsworthy, shareable, clickable) that in today's media reward outrage and identity over truth. Transport is a cost nobody chooses; selection is a choice somebody makes, which is why it's the half of the machine that can be steered, and why *who owns the gates* turns out to be the whole question. Where it lands, once the reading instructions are stripped: the same claim can arrive roughly right, or inverted into something false with nobody lying, or emptied into a flag for which side you're on. The book's prescription is not to retreat into smaller like-minded groups (that curdles into echo chambers) but to build *bridges*: deep experts trained to translate across worldviews, and durable institutions funded outside the attention market that stay usable even when trust is gone.
