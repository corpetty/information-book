## 1. Argument

**Thesis.** The stretch where specialist findings get retold for everyone else (the News and Meme stages, staffed by journalists, popularizers, influencers, educators and AI summarizers) is where most visible distortion happens, because the retellers answer to audiences and employers rather than to the specialists, so they reshape on purpose against attention metrics.

**Does it earn it?** Only the first third argues. L15–17 locates the zone, L21–29 makes the one genuine move (this is *active* reshaping against incentives, not passive blur), L31–45 maps the population. After L47 the chapter stops arguing and starts pointing: three "worked cases" (L51–55) that are not worked (no date, no headline, no paper, no named outlet), then five sections (L67–91, ~1,300 words) each of the form "Ch N's argument applies here too." The chapter's own uncertainty section confesses the problem twice: the cases flatter the specialist side (L109) and the "real human costs" are asserted, not shown (L115). Those are not uncertainties; they are known defects in the body.

Loops: "angriest chapter" at L7, L11, L97. The definition of the zone at L15 is restated at L101 in bold. L93–97 ("What this chapter is doing") is a table of contents written after the fact, and L99–103 does it again.

**Rewrites.**

*L9 (original ~190 words):* "Chapter 6, Part III. The bridge zone. Earlier chapters worked the abstract structure …"
> **Rewrite (~70 words):** The first five chapters were about structure: how ideas get compressed and which ones get through. This one is about a place. Between the specialists who know something and the rest of us sits a crowd of people whose job is the handoff: journalists, popularizers, influencers, teachers off their home turf, and now AI summarizers. That stretch is where most of the damage you can actually see gets done.

*L25 (original ~150 words):* "The distinction matters because it tells you why bridge-zone failure modes look so different …"
> **Rewrite (~75 words):** Upstream failures look like honest people getting it wrong with the tools they had: a contaminated measurement, a biased sample, a paper that fooled its reviewers. Bridge-zone failures look different. The operator usually isn't wrong about the finding. They're right about what their audience will click, and they compress toward that. They are compressing correctly against their real incentives, and their real incentives aren't the finding's structure.

*L71 (original ~230 words):* "The capture taxonomy adds substrate-level precision to this …"
> **Rewrite (~90 words):** Two things can get captured here. The gate: what editors and algorithms reward. And the audience: what years of engagement-shaped news have trained readers to expect. The second is worse, because it lives in people. An audience trained to expect the punchy version rejects the hedged one as boring or "not journalism," and at that point the bridge zone is locked into the punchy shape no matter what any individual reporter wants. Audience and operators have co-evolved into the same equilibrium [[political-economy-of-attention|Chapter 10]] describes at platform scale.

*L101 (original ~130-word bold block):* "The chapter, said plain: **the bridge zone is the pipeline stage …**"
> **Rewrite (~55 words, unbolded):** Where I land: the bridge zone is a population, not a mechanism. Its people answer to audiences and employers, not to the fields they translate. So the reshaping is deliberate and it optimizes for attention. That's where the book's structural argument turns into things you have actually been misinformed about, and where any fix has to land.
