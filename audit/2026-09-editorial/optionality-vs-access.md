# Audit: `content/optionality vs access.md` (Ch 4) — 3,723 words

## 1. Argument

**Thesis.** Every artifact that carries an idea sits on one curve: the more of a domain's precision it keeps (optionality), the fewer people can pick it up (access), and the curve exists because receivers have finite budgets.

**Does it earn it?** Mostly, and earlier than the chapter admits. The camera paragraph (l.9) states the whole trade-off; the five domain walks (l.11–118) are illustration, and the payoff (l.124: the curve is the receiver budget "reflected in the artifact's design") is one paragraph. Everything after l.124 is restatement or forward reference. Specific sags:

- l.7 is a false start ("The words I've just used in that last sentence don't do justice"). l.9 is the real opening.
- Each domain section ends by announcing "the same curve" (l.82, 88, 90, 92, 98, 102, 106, 110, 122: nine times). The reader gets it by the second domain.
- l.100 hangs the politics section on "the *integration project*, the book's eventual prescription (Ch 9)" and "Part IV": two terms the reader has no way to hold five chapters early.
- l.108 does the same with "bridge-node argument from Part IV" and "curation gates".
- l.120–144 is three endings: "What the curve actually is," the bullets tying it to Ch 5/8/11, and "Where I land," which restates l.124 in bold.
- l.132–138 ("What the curve is not") is a well-written distinction, but it is a footnote to a separate essay and links out of the book (l.136) rather than to `content/concepts/abyss.md`.

**Single most valuable structural change.** Merge l.120–130 and l.140–144 into one closing section of ~250 words (the l.124 claim, one sentence per domain on how the trade is institutionalized, one sentence each for Ch 5/8/11). Cut l.7 and open on the camera. That alone removes ~700 words and every "the chapter names the curve" sentence.
