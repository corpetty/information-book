# Audit: `content/transport-vs-selection.md` (2,649 words)

## 1. Argument

**Thesis (mine):** Transport (lossy re-encoding) and selection (criterial gating) are co-equal, parallel mechanisms; they differ in *modality* (a cost vs. a choice), and only a choice can be aimed, captured, or repaired, which is why the book centers selection.

**Does it earn it?** Yes, but only in the last 20 lines. The file is a palimpsest: L7–82 argue "selection is primary" at length (five test cases, four reasons, seven chapter consequences); L93–101 add the want-loop; L103–118 *retract* "primary" and land on modality; L120–123 restate the landing. A reader spends 100 lines being persuaded of a claim the author then withdraws at L112 ("So I'm dropping *primary*"). The test cases (L21–49) remain the best evidence in the note that selection "has a what-it-rewards," but they are currently framed as evidence for the abandoned ranking.

**Depends on terms the reader doesn't have:** *preconditions* (L95, home Ch 5), *receiver budget* (L89, home Ch 3), *manipulation surface* (L74), *option space* (L116, defined only in Ch 5b), *runtime gate* (L122).

**Single most valuable structural change:** invert the note. Open with the current commitment (L114–118, L122), then run the five test cases as evidence that selection has criteria and transport does not, then the want-loop (L97) and the historical claim (L55). Delete "Where I land" (L51–65) and "What this changes for the book" (L66–82): Ch 5b already restates generate/bound/pick and modality in full (`selection-as-other-engine.md:17,25,39`), so the "rewrites several chapters" section is a to-do list that has been done.

**Rewrites.**

*L9 (original, 150 words):* "A quick note on the word *selection*. The term is loaded. It carries connotations from natural selection on one side … Keep the word, distrust the connotation, attend to which kind of selection is at work in each specific case."
*Rewrite (60 words):* "One word of caution about *selection*. It sounds like Darwin on one side and like a gatekeeper on the other. I mean the mechanism both share: something ranks candidates against criteria and passes only some forward. An algorithm does it without anyone deciding; an editor does it on purpose. Same mechanism, and I'll say which kind is running each time it matters."

*L15 (original, 145 words):* "**Selection view.** Information enters an attention ecosystem. Receivers don't pass everything they get … what they choose to pass is what the selection view is pointing at."
*Rewrite (70 words):* "**Selection view.** Receivers don't pass on everything they receive. They pass what's worth passing, for their own reasons: it moved them, it flatters their side, it tells them what to do. What survives is whatever wins that contest, and winners tend to be compact. The telephone game hides this because the rules force every child to pass the sentence. Real networks have no such rule."

*L105–112 (original, ~230 words):* "One word in this note has been doing too much work: *primary*. 'Selection is primary' was really four claims wearing one coat. 1. … 4. … So I'm dropping *primary*."
*Rewrite (80 words):* "I used to say selection is *primary*. That word hid four claims. One is false: selection is not causally upstream of transport. Two are true but historical: selection is the constraint that binds now, because transport got cheap. One is true and not historical at all: selection is where manipulation and repair have to act. Only the last needs the book's weight, and it isn't a ranking. So: no more *primary*."

*L118 (original, ~140 words):* "And that puts the medium in a harder light than [[medium-and-manipulation]] left it … if the option space was built so the long, careful argument cannot form."
*Rewrite (75 words):* "The medium that writes the menu is doing selection too; it just did it once, in advance. A 280-character limit was somebody's decision. To everyone living inside it, it is the shape of the world. That makes the medium the strongest gate and the least visible one. You never have to suppress the long careful argument if the menu was built so it can't form."
