# Audit: `content/political-economy-of-attention.md` (Ch 10, ~8,150 words)

## 1. Argument

**Thesis (my words):** The dominant medium's selection gates are tuned to maximize engagement because that is what monetizes; this "self-capture" is more stable than any outside attacker because there is no one to fight, only an arrangement; and the same equilibrium reappears wherever a medium's frozen defaults make watching people cheap (transparent blockchains being the second instance).

**Does it earn it?** Mostly, but at nearly twice the length it needs. The argument is fully stated by the end of "Two kinds of capture" (line ~71). Everything after that either (a) adds a second instance (money, lines 73–87, ~1,300 words), (b) defends against objections (lines 99–125, ~1,400 words), (c) restates for the rest of the book (lines 127–143, ~720 words), or (d) lists doubts (lines 145–165, ~900 words). That is ~4,300 words, over half the chapter, spent after the argument has been made. The chapter restates its own thesis in bold at least five times (lines 33, 67, 87, 139, 143).

Loops: "no villain / no enemy / nothing to organize against" appears at lines 7, 41, 53, 67, 79, 81, 95, 139. "Zero marginal cost of attention / priced out" at 93, 95, 131, 139. Ch 8's asymmetric collapse is re-explained at 9, 59, 91, 95, 97, 133, 161.

Terms the reader doesn't yet have: "frozen selection" and "option space" are used from line 15 onward as if settled, with the definition deferred to a wikilink. "Objective shadow" (line 77), "the note's third loop outcome", "captured cell" (line 59), "identity-flag-flavored" (line 69), "heavy votes" (line 77), "money in 1971" (line 85) all lean on other notes.

**Single most valuable structural change:** Move "Pressure-tests on the modality argument" (lines 99–125) out of the chapter into an appendix/note (the book already has a "contested claims" apparatus in `data/claims.json`), keeping only one paragraph in the main text: the two refinements (neutral drift; dispositions pre-exist) and the Mercier timescale answer. Rationale: this is the author arguing with his reading list in front of the reader; the one thing it establishes (the argument depends on medium-as-frozen-selection) is already stated at 19–21 and again at 143. Cutting it removes ~1,200 words and the chapter's most academic register. Secondary: merge "What this changes for the book" into "Where I land" and cut the bullet list.

### Four rewrites

**L11 (192 words with L9).** Original: "I should be honest about why they punt. The structural machinery of the book (transport, selection, the three operations, the medium as frozen selection, the truth-compression regimes, the capture-asymmetry) is morally neutral on its face. It explains *how* information moves and *what* the failure modes are, but it does not name *who benefits when the gates are tuned the way they are*. That naming is this chapter's job, and the book has structurally committed to having to do it. As soon as you say selection is criterial and criteria are set, you have made the question of *who sets the criteria* unavoidable. The political economy is not a side chapter the book gets to choose; it is the consequence of where the earlier chapters landed."

Rewrite: "Everything so far has been machinery: how ideas get compressed, which gates they pass, why the medium matters. Machinery has no interests. But the moment you say a gate works by criteria, and criteria have to be set, you have to ask who sets them and who benefits. This chapter is that question."

**L31.** Original: "Naively this looks like it breaks any selection story: if selection reflected what people wanted, the unwanted thing would lose. It is not a break. It is the frozen-selection point caught in the act of operation. What spreads diverges from what receivers want because the gate that decided was not the receiver's gate. It was the medium's, the algorithm, the option space, the recommendation system, tuned by someone whose interest is not the receiver's. [[misinformation-age|*The Misinformation Age*]] (p.173) gives the live mechanism: '[fake news] is amplified by algorithms...' The amplification is not a side effect; it is the point of the system. The system is doing what it was tuned to do."

Rewrite: "If selection tracked what people wanted, the unwanted content would lose. It doesn't lose, because the gate doing the choosing isn't yours. It's the platform's, and the platform is tuned by someone whose interest is not yours. O'Connor and Weatherall put the mechanism plainly: the algorithms surface 'which stories are highly engaging so as to maximize their viewership' ([[misinformation-age]], p.173). That isn't a malfunction. It's the system working."

**L67.** Original (155 words): "The chapter's load-bearing structural claim: **self-capture is more stable than external capture, and that is what makes it the central problem.** External capture has an enemy. You can name them, fight them, regulate against them; the polarization-via-distrust arms race is real and hostile, but it is *contestable* because the adversary is identifiable. Self-capture has no enemy. There is no one to defeat, because the captured agent is the institution operating normally. The equilibrium *is* the institution's regular business; the captured state and the functional state are the same state. **You can fight a captor; you cannot fight an equilibrium.** This is the deepest reason proposals to reform the platforms from within keep underperforming: they target an adversary that does not exist, and leave the equilibrium untouched. Self-capture has to be either *displaced*... or *dismantled*..., which is to say, structurally, by removing the cost-shifting that lets it stay invisible."

Rewrite: "Self-capture is the harder problem because it has no enemy. An outside attacker can be named, sued, regulated, exposed. A business model can't; the captured state and the normal state are the same state. You can fight a captor; you cannot fight an equilibrium. That's why reforming platforms from within keeps underperforming: it aims at an adversary that isn't there. The only moves left are to build a selection arrangement outside the equilibrium, or to change the money flows that hold it in place."

**L95.** Original (170 words). Rewrite: "A reader with a fixed budget and no line item for 'checking quality' takes the free option, which is also the one engineered to be the most engaging. That isn't a failing; it's the market they were handed, priced at zero for engagement and at hours for depth. The journal, the reporter, the textbook aren't outargued or discredited. They're priced out. So the deep form keeps getting produced and nobody's attention is left to reach it: exactly the asymmetry [[preservation-vs-training|Chapter 8]] described, produced by cost-shifting plus engagement pricing."
