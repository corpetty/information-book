## 1. Argument

**Thesis (mine):** As a network grows, its binding myth must compress; compression widens how many ways the myth can be read (interpretive latitude); latitude, sorted by community and by identity-rewarding gates, segments the network; bureaucracy is the institution that re-supplies the dropped key, and whether the loop integrates, fragments, or is captured depends on how capturable the bureaucracy is.

**Does it earn it?** The chain L12–43 earns itself link by link. The problem is repetition and one dangling seam. The loop is stated in full at L9, L65, and L67, and again in the chapters that cite it (`preservation-vs-training.md:11,63`, `integration-problem.md:21`, `political-economy-of-attention.md:59`). L45–61 ("What decides the loop") is the note's most original section and 40% of its length; the three outcomes (L53–57) are buried in prose, and L59 is a 150-word sentence containing a rhetorical Q&A ("Does that not just push the problem up a level…? It does, once, and then it bottoms out"). L47 opens "I left the loop a section ago with an open end: does it compound or brake itself?" — no preceding section asks that; it refers to a deleted draft. ⚠ L85 is a paragraph pasted in from the intersubjective note.

**Depends on terms the reader doesn't have:** *credibly neutral* (L59, from Ch 5's Bitcoin case, no gloss); *orthogonal regime* (L31, home 5c); *preservation vs. training* (L43, home Ch 8, which this note precedes in logic).

**Rewrites.**

*L15–17 (original, ~200 words):* "Both framings quietly assume the receiver reaches for *one* particular key … because the spread is what does the structural work."
*Rewrite (85 words):* "Both accounts assume the receiver reaches for one particular wrong key. Drop that. Strip the key and the receiver has *no* key, and a claim with no key attached supports many readings. **Compression doesn't pin a wrong reading; it unpins the reading.** Call the range of readings a claim will bear its *interpretive latitude*. 'The Earth goes around the Sun' has narrow latitude. 'Freedom,' 'follow the science,' 'X is real' have enormous latitude: each will carry a dozen incompatible readings without strain."

*L39 (original, ~230 words):* "Segments pulling apart need something to hold them, and Harari already named it … the more of its cohesion has to shift from myth to bureaucracy."
*Rewrite (90 words):* "Something has to hold the segments together, and Harari names it: bureaucracy. I use the word narrowly (see the glossary) for the institution that keeps the full form alive and re-teaches people to read it. Real bureaucracies also tax, coerce, and record; those are out of scope here. What I add to Harari is the arrow. Myth and bureaucracy aren't two pillars a network happens to have. **Bureaucracy grows to cover the gap that scale tore in the myth**, and the bigger the network, the more of its cohesion moves from the one to the other."

*L57 (original, ~200 words):* "**A bureaucracy that is effective but captured** is the third outcome, and the one a two-outcome story misses … folding them into one is what hides this cell."
*Rewrite (85 words):* "**Effective but captured.** The bureaucracy still re-supplies a key, but a tuned one, the reading that serves whoever holds it. This is worse than failure because from outside it looks like success: the network scales. The witch-hunting apparatus of the late fifteenth century was an extraordinarily *effective* key-supply machine, propagating one canonical reading across an enormous network, and by this book's lights it was tuning that key against truth. Effectiveness and incorruptibility are different axes. Fold them together and this cell disappears."

*L61 (original, ~230 words):* "All three outcomes are descriptive … and it is the real brief this note hands to Part IV."
*Rewrite (80 words):* "Of the three, capture is the stable one. A captured bureaucracy produces scale, looks like success, and contains nothing that forces its own correction, so it is probably most of what we see. That's what any prescription is up against. You cannot build an institution that can't be captured. You can build one where capture can't quietly hold: one that, once captured, fails fast and visibly instead of running on as an instrument of control."
