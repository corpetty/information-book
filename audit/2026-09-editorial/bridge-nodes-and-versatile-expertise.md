## 1. Argument

**Thesis (mine):** The person who can carry complex truth between two fields is not a generalist but a deep specialist who has also trained the habit of suspending their own frame; the four ways expertise fails are the individual-level face of polarization-via-distrust, so the same capacity fixes both.

**Does it earn it?** Half. L11–43 (why generalists fail; what depth plus flexibility buys) is earned. The identity claim at L59 ("**The curse of expertise is the cognitive substrate of polarization-via-distrust.** Same mechanism, two vocabularies") is asserted by juxtaposition. O'Connor and Weatherall's result is about how agents *weight evidence by source trust*; the four failure modes are about *perceptual chunking*. They are analogous and probably causally linked, not the same mechanism; L61 then builds the note's conclusion on the identity ("That collapses the integration problem and the bridge-node problem into a single question"). Either argue the link (discounting outside evidence is what paradigm lock-in *does* at the individual level; aggregate it and you get the network result) or downgrade to "the individual-level mechanism the network result aggregates."

Second problem: L47–57 block-quotes the author's own blog post four times (~150 words) and L19 calls that post "primary source." It is the author's prose; write it here. L63 imports a paragraph from a second essay (the abyss) and ships the reader off-site.

**Rewrites.**

*L15 (original, ~120 words):* "It doesn't survive two pressures. The first is operational: statistical literacy doesn't actually let you read a particle-physics paper…"
*Rewrite (60 words):* "Two things break it. First, statistical literacy doesn't let you read a particle-physics paper. The problem isn't vocabulary. An expert sees the argument as a few large shapes; a generalist reads it sentence by sentence, holds nothing as a unit, and runs out of working memory before it resolves. More critical reading doesn't close that gap. The expert is perceiving differently."

*L39 (original, ~140 words):* "The cognitive substrate has two layers. The first is depth…"
*Rewrite (65 words):* "A bridge needs two things. Depth: the trained perception that lets them see the other side's arguments at all, and that gets them recognized as a peer. And a second habit on top of the depth: looking for structural analogies, suspending their own frame, treating their paradigm as one option. Depth alone is a specialist stuck at home. The habit alone is a generalist nobody admits."

*L57–59 (original, ~230 words):* "Stop and look at this list. These are not four separate ways expertise can fail…"
*Rewrite (90 words):* "Read the four together and they describe one person: someone *rationally* discounting evidence from outside their paradigm. Lock-in makes outside evidence look unserious; filtering makes it hard to see; overconfidence says no need to look harder; rigidity makes the outsider's reframing sound like nonsense. O'Connor and Weatherall's polarization-via-distrust is what you get when a network full of such people weighs each other's evidence. I read the four failure modes as the individual mechanism that result aggregates. I have not shown they are identical."

*L63 (original, ~160 words):* "There is an experiential version of where that flexibility comes from, which I work through separately in [the abyss]…"
*Rewrite (55 words):* "Where does the second habit come from? Partly from having seen how large your own field is. The [[abyss|abyss]] argument is that overconfidence is only available to a specialist who hasn't yet looked over that edge. Lock-in loosens once you know your paradigm is one island; cross-application cools once you've felt how little you command at home."
