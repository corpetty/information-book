**Rewrites.**

*L11 (original ~160 words):* "The chapter has to be careful in two specific places …"
> **Rewrite (~70 words):** Two ways this goes wrong. Said badly, "raw emotion is the floor" is either obvious (anyone who has used social media for an hour knows it) or cynicism in a lab coat. I want the structural version: an equilibrium of named mechanisms, not a verdict on human nature. And Hugo Mercier's *Not Born Yesterday* argues that people are much harder to manipulate than this story needs. I take that seriously below. I think the argument survives it, narrowly and in a specific place.

*L43 (original ~230 words, six stacked quotes):* "Mercier's case, with the extracted quotes as anchors …"
> **Rewrite (~90 words):** Mercier's case: humans evolved *open vigilance*, a paired system that is open to useful information and on guard against unreliable sources (pp. 31–42). Our errors run toward not trusting when we should, not the reverse. And the record of mass persuasion is a record of failure: "the vast majority of mass persuasion efforts … end in abject failure" (p. 259). Even Nazi propaganda worked only where it confirmed what people already believed, and "failed abysmally" where it didn't (p. 131). If that's right, a story in which the system floods us with emotional junk and the junk sticks has a serious objection to answer.

*L69 (original ~190 words):* "The captured-equilibrium and justification-market may not be cleanly separable …"
> **Rewrite (~65 words):** I can't cleanly separate two things. Chapter 10 says the platform's business model tunes gates toward identity and threat. Mercier says people shop for content that justifies what they'd already decided. Those might be one mechanism seen from two ends: the gates shape what people come to demand, and what people demand is what the gates are tuned against. I flag it and leave it open.

*L136 (original ~220 words, all bold):* "The chapter, said plain: **the propagation equilibrium of mass networks …**"
> **Rewrite (~80 words, unbolded):** Where I land: once a network is big enough and its gates have rewarded engagement long enough, what reliably spreads settles at raw emotion. The floor depends on the medium, is anchored in what human attention evolved to notice, and is tuned toward the corrosive end of the set by the current business model. Most of what it produces is belief held at arm's length, not conviction. Mercier is right that single campaigns mostly fail; my worry is what decades do.
