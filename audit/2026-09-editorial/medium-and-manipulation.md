# Audit: `content/medium-and-manipulation.md` (2,685 words)

## 1. Argument

**Thesis (mine):** The medium is not a pipe under selection; it *is* the selection criteria. It acts on every gate three ways (what can fit, what wins, what receivers learn to want), manipulation is tuning a gate's criteria against truth, and a medium preserves truth only if its criteria correlate with truth *and* its want-loop doesn't erode the appetite for it.

**Does it earn it?** The core (L11–49) does. The problems are structural. (1) L33 calls capacity "the most mechanical and the least interesting"; L63–69 then says "I want to take that back … I had it upside down." The reader is walked through a ranking and then its reversal; the fix is to write the three levers once, in the corrected order. (2) L41 ends with a literal `[NEED EVIDENCE HERE]` marker in published prose. (3) "What this changes for the book" (L71–83) is a to-do list that has since been executed: 5b names the tuner (`selection-as-other-engine.md:55`), 5c cashes the "truth-correlated" promissory note (`truth-compression-and-when-each-wins.md:57`), Ch 7 states the floor as medium-relative (`emotional-memetics.md:93`), Ch 10 uses the manipulation definition (`political-economy-of-attention.md:65`).

**Depends on terms the reader doesn't have:** *preconditions* (L39), *Chapter 5b* (L27, cited before it exists in the reading order), *Mercier's challenge* (L81, home Ch 7), *Postman's technology/medium distinction* (never cited, though L11–17 *is* that distinction; see §3).

**Single most valuable change:** merge L63–69 into L29–41 (capacity last, as the deepest lever, no retraction), delete L71–83, and cite Postman for the "medium is not the substrate" move so the note's opening reads as a position in a known conversation rather than a private discovery.

**Rewrites.**

*L15–17 (original, ~150 words):* "I think that picture is wrong, and getting it wrong is the reason 'the medium' keeps showing up as an afterthought … changing them is what changing the medium *is*."
*Rewrite (55 words):* "That picture is wrong. The medium isn't under the gate; it sets the gate's terms. Every stage of the pipeline has a medium, and what that medium can hold and rewards is what the gate at that stage selects for. Change the medium and you have changed the criteria. That's not a side effect. It's the same act."

*L37 (original, ~120 words):* "This is also where *manipulation* enters with a precise definition. Manipulation, in this book's frame, is **tuning a gate's criteria against truth.** Lying doesn't qualify…"
*Rewrite (60 words):* "Here is what I mean by *manipulation*: **tuning a gate's criteria against truth.** A lie is content; it can be checked. Manipulation sits above content. It changes what the gate rewards so that true things lose by construction. A medium is manipulable when its criteria are cheap to tune and tuned by someone who doesn't care about truth."

*L47 (original, ~330 words):* "So the claim has to be stated at the right strength, with a time-scale caveat … Part IV will have to commit on which scale matters."
*Rewrite (110 words):* "So the claim has to be sized to the evidence. Over a year, a medium doesn't invent wants. It picks among the ones we already have (threat, belonging, status) and hands one the megaphone. That's a casting director, not an author, and it's still an enormous power. Over a generation I suspect it does more: a cohort raised inside one amplification pattern may want things no earlier cohort wanted, because what got amplified in their formative years is what they learned to want. I can't show that yet. The casting-director claim is what this note rests on."

*L65–67 (original, ~200 words):* "The section above ranked the three things a medium does … Selection then runs, visibly and honestly, on a menu that was rigged before it sat down."
*Rewrite (as the corrected L33, 70 words):* "**One: it draws the menu.** A gate picks from a set of variants; it never makes the set. The medium decides which forms an idea can take at all. The 280-character limit doesn't slow the long argument down; it takes it off the menu. Selection then runs, honestly, on a menu rigged before it sat down. This is the deepest of the three levers, and the least visible."
