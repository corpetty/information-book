# Audit: `content/case-studies-and-three-realities.md` (Chapter 2)

5,922 words, twice Chapter 1. Two chapters welded together: four case studies (28–82, the good half) and a scope note about Harari's three realities (84–150) that has grown a fifth story, a "what this changes for the book" section, and a "where I land" that restates the whole thing. Roughly a third of the file is the book talking about itself.

## 1. Argument

**Thesis:** Ideas fail in the pipeline in a direction, not at random: a correction is always less dramatic, flattering, and useful than the myth it replaces, so gates that reward those things pass the myth and drop the correction; and for things that are real only because we agree on them (money, law), the pipeline doesn't transmit truth but makes it.

**Does it earn it?** The four cases earn the first half. Each is well-chosen, and the "what this case shows" bolds are genuinely the argument. The synthesis at 80 ("the pipeline doesn't break at random. It fails in a *direction*") is the best sentence in the file and should be the chapter's spine.

The second half does not earn its length. 84–96 introduces three realities; 98–112 walks money; 114–132 says what it changes; 134–138 restates; 140–150 restates again with chapter numbers; 152–164 caveats. The intersubjective machinery is repeatedly deferred to the `intersubjective-truth` note (112, 126, 132, 144, 154), so the chapter is telling the reader four times where the argument lives instead of making it.

**Loops/restatements:** The purpose is stated at 9, again at 11, again at 13 ("Two reasons for the chapter, in other words"). The four-exit taxonomy is stated at 19–24, then per-case, then at 78, then at 136, then at 142, then at 150.

**Terms the reader doesn't have:** "curation fitness"/"gate-fitness" (38, 60, 62, 70, 78, 82, 138), "political economy" (48, 78), "failure surface" (82, 136), "decoding key" (118, 126, 128), "consumer-key substrate" (148), "capture" (118–130), "capture-asymmetry the chapter named" (128 — it didn't). All belong to Ch 5c/8/10.

**Single most valuable structural change:** Split. Keep 7–82 plus a compressed 84–96 and the money walk (98–110) as Chapter 2, ending on 80. Move 114–150 wholesale into `intersubjective-truth.md` (which already owns the machinery) or delete the parts that duplicate it. The chapter drops to ~3,500 words and ends on its own best paragraph.

### Four paragraph rewrites

**1. Line 11 (original, 205 words):**
> Chapter 2, Part I. [[the-information-landscape|Chapter 1]] walked through the pipeline abstractly: the transport pipeline (The Out There → ...) stage by stage, then the sibling selection pipeline with one *gate* per stage. (A gate, in plain terms, is...) The abstract picture is correct but loose. Ch 2 brings it to ground in two ways. First, by walking specific findings... Second, by being explicit about which kind of reality is in transit... Harari names that third category cleanly in [[nexus-book|Nexus]], and the rest of the book quietly assumes a reality type without saying which. This chapter pulls the assumption out into the open.

Rewrite (74 words):
> [[the-information-landscape|Chapter 1]] drew the pipeline in the abstract. Here I walk four real findings through it, stage by stage, to see what each gate actually does. Then I ask a question the diagram skips: what *kind* of thing is moving? A hormone result and a currency both travel the same pipe, but only one of them has a fact outside the pipe to check against. That difference changes what "failure" even means.

(Delete 13 entirely.)

**2. Line 48 (original, 232 words):**
> What this case shows: **the consensus stage can and does work.** The book's diagnostic chapters mostly engage cases where the consensus gate failed... Without this counterexample the book's posture would read as universal pessimism... The diagnostic question for the rest of the book is not "do the gates ever work" (they sometimes do) but *what conditions* make consensus catch errors versus pass them, and the conditions are visibly worse in social-science high-public-interest domains (where Power Posing lives) than in hard-physical-science extraordinary-claims domains (where arsenic-life lived). The political economy [[Chapter 10]] diagnoses is what makes the difference.

Rewrite (96 words):
> **The consensus gate can work.** Arsenic-life is the counterexample to power posing: same year, same kind of splashy publication, opposite outcome. So the question isn't whether the gates ever catch errors. It's what makes them catch one claim and wave another through. Part of the answer is visible already. An extraordinary claim in biochemistry has a bacterium that any lab can re-test. A modest claim in social psychology, with a TED talk attached, has a story that people want to be true. The gate faces different pressure in each case, and Chapter 10 is about where that pressure comes from.

**3. Line 82 (original, 141 words):**
> That phrasing matters because it tells you what the prescription has to be designed against. Building integration infrastructure that "fixes the pipeline" or "tells the truth louder" misreads the diagnosis. The pipeline tells truth fine when the gates are tuned to reward truth-correlated form; the pipeline distributes manufactured content fine when the gates are tuned to reward form regardless of provenance; the pipeline persistently fails corrective signal even when the corrective is correct because correctives have systematically worse gate-fitness. The four cases are four samples drawn from one continuous failure surface, and the surface's shape is what the book's later chapters are diagnosing in formal terms.

Rewrite (58 words):
> This matters for what you'd build. "Tell the truth louder" doesn't help, because volume isn't the problem; the gates are. A pipeline whose gates reward drama will carry drama whether it's true or not, and will drop a correction whether it's right or not. Any fix has to change what the gates reward. That is the rest of the book.

**4. Line 136 (original, 178 words, all bold):**
> The chapter, said whole: **the pipeline's abstract structure (Ch 1) becomes concrete through case studies... Being explicit about which reality is in transit is the chapter's second contribution, and it sharpens what the later chapters are arguing about.**

Rewrite (70 words):
> Four findings, four exits. Power posing ran the whole pipe and its correction never caught it. Arsenic-life died where it should have. The Stanford Prison Experiment's correction is sitting in the journals, unread past them. Astrology never entered the pipe and lives at its far end anyway. One pattern under all four: correction loses to myth wherever the gate rewards what myths have and corrections lack. And for money, there is no correction at all, only a new agreement.
