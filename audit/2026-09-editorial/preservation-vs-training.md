# Audit: `content/preservation-vs-training.md` (Ch 8, 5,063 words)

## 1. Argument

**Thesis.** What the book has been calling "bureaucracy" is really two institutional jobs, keeping the full form alive somewhere (preservation) and keeping people able to read it (training); each alone fails, and under capture or neglect the training half does more damage and heals slower, so it is the half to defend first.

**Does it earn it?** The core (L15–19, L25–31, L35–45, L53–57, L69–81) does. The two-function split, the three failure modes, the "inaccessible archive vs. competence pointed at the wrong target" contrast, and the capture/decay asymmetries are real argument. What sags:

- The asymmetry thesis is stated six times (L69 bold, L71, L73 last sentence, L79 bold, L81, L111 bold) and its only *evidence*, the Soviet and Reformation cases, is exiled to the uncertainty section (L121) where it is framed as a reason not to trust the thesis.
- The dilution loop from the myth note is re-summarized three times (L11, L15, L63).
- Two mid-chapter "I want to flag" detours (L43, L83) each duplicate an uncertainty bullet (L119, L123).
- L97–113 (933 words) restate the chapter and pre-announce Chs 9–12; nothing new is argued.
- Terms the reader doesn't have yet: "consumer-key substrate" and "surface substrate" (L69) arrive cold from the capture-taxonomy note; "chunked" is used at L37 before being explained at L39.

**Single most valuable structural change.** Move the L121 cases (Soviet science, Reformation theology) into the body immediately after L73 as the *test* of the capture asymmetry, then cut L97–113 entirely and let the chapter end on L95 (the chokepoint-with-rents beneficiary), which is already the hand-off to Ch 10. The chapter would go from six restatements and zero tests to one statement and one test.

### Four rewrites

**L9 (original, 143 words):** "Chapter 8, Part IV. The first prescriptive chapter of the rebuild. It pairs with [[integration-problem|Chapter 9]] but precedes it: Chapter 9 asks what kind of agent … The answer is what Harari, in [[nexus-book|Nexus]], handed the book as *bureaucracy* … The word, on inspection, is doing two jobs at once inside even that narrow reading, and the rest of Part IV cannot be argued cleanly until they are pulled apart."

*Rewrite (61 words):* "The last chapter ended with a name and not much else. When a network outgrows its myth, something has to carry the dropped key back to people, and following Harari I called that something *bureaucracy*, meaning only the repair job, none of the taxing and filing. This chapter opens the word up. There are two jobs inside it, and they are not the same job."

**L19 (original, 96 words):** "These are two operations, not one. Hold the complex form somewhere; install the capacity to read it in receivers. The first is **preservation**. The second is **training**. The chapter, said plain: **'bureaucracy' was the name for what is really a pair of distinct institutional functions, and they have to be done by different kinds of institution at once: one to hold the un-compressed form alive, one to keep producing receivers who can read it. Either alone fails, and they fail differently when starved and differently when captured.**"

*Rewrite (52 words):* "Call the first job **preservation**: somewhere, the full form stays alive. Call the second **training**: somebody keeps producing people who can read it. Different institutions usually do them, and they fail in different ways. The rest of this chapter is about those differences, because they decide where to spend when the money runs short."

**L47 (original, 108 words):** "This chapter has a contemporary institutional ally on exactly this prescriptive line. Pope Leo XIV's *Magnifica Humanitas* (May 2026; Ch 11 engages it at length) calls for *'an educational alliance for the digital age'* … The encyclical's framing is institutionally significant (papal teaching documents are not casual interventions) and grounds the book's training-side prescription in a contemporary cross-tradition conversation rather than leaving it floating…"

*Rewrite (38 words):* "I am not alone in putting schools first. Leo XIV's *Magnifica Humanitas* (2026) calls for *'an educational alliance for the digital age'* against digital manipulation, which is the training function under another name. Chapter 11 takes the encyclical up properly."

**L89 (original, 154 words):** "I want to commit to that provisionally and on instinct, because the alternative diagnosis is significantly less actionable. Universities are a worked case in either direction. The research side has held, with adjustments … The pattern is not 'the institution has rotted.' It is 'the *training half* has rotted, while the preservation half persists, and from outside the institution still looks alive because the preservation half is what advertises.'"

*Rewrite (84 words):* "Take universities. The research side still runs: journals come out, labs publish, the form keeps being extended. The teaching side is where the damage is. Most instruction is now done by contingent staff who cannot mentor the way a career faculty could, and the credential has quietly replaced the training it was supposed to certify. From outside, the university looks healthy, because the research side is the part that advertises. Inside, the half that makes new readers is the half that has gone."
